# MLA-C02 Exam-Day Guide

This page covers the logistics of the AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer - Associate (MLA-C02) exam: how to book it, how the two delivery options differ, what to have ready, how the exam is built and scored, and what happens after you finish. MLA-C02 is new and, at the time of writing, still in its beta period, so several details differ between the beta and the standard (general availability) exam. Every fact below links to the official AWS page it came from. AWS updates its policies from time to time, so confirm anything time-sensitive on the linked page before your appointment.

> Also see the [MLA-C02 practice questions](https://www.savemycert.com/practice/aws-machine-learning-engineer-associate/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=mla-c02-study-guide) and [timed mock exams](https://www.savemycert.com/mocks/aws-machine-learning-engineer-associate/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=mla-c02-study-guide).

## Beta vs standard exam

MLA-C02 replaces MLA-C01. AWS opened registration for the MLA-C02 beta on September 1, 2026, and beta delivery began on September 29, 2026. The last day to take MLA-C01 in English was September 28, 2026. MLA-C01 in Japanese, Korean, and Simplified Chinese remains available until MLA-C02 reaches general availability, and the general availability dates are listed as "TBD" on the official certification page.

|           | MLA-C02 beta                                | MLA-C01 (the standard format AWS still lists) |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Exam code | ME1-C02                                     | MLA-C01                                       |
| Questions | 85, multiple choice and multiple response   | 65, multiple choice and multiple response     |
| Time      | 170 minutes                                 | 130 minutes                                   |
| Price     | 75 USD (beta pricing)                       | 150 USD                                       |
| Languages | English only (more at general availability) | Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese          |

AWS has not yet published the duration and price of the standard MLA-C02 exam. The MLA-C02 exam guide gives the standard format as 50 scored plus 15 unscored questions. Associate exams are priced at 150 USD in AWS's FAQs, but check the certification page for the MLA-C02 figure once general availability is announced.

About betas in general: AWS uses beta exams to validate questions before they are used on standard exams. A beta contains more total questions and more scored questions than the standard version, runs for a limited time (generally one to three months), and is offered at a discount. If you pass the beta, you earn the certification. You can take the beta only once.

Source: [AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer - Associate](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-machine-learning-engineer-associate/), [Before Testing](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/before-testing/), [AWS Certification FAQs](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/faqs/)

## Booking the exam

To register, sign in to aws.training, open Certification in the top navigation, choose your AWS Certification Account, then Schedule New Exam. Select the exam and continue to Pearson VUE, the test delivery provider, to finish registration. You need an AWS Certification Account before you can schedule.

AWS lists local-currency prices for some currencies and notes that taxes such as VAT may apply. See the FAQs for the current foreign exchange details.

If your plans change, you can reschedule up to 24 hours before your appointment, and each appointment can be rescheduled only twice. Cancellations must also happen at least 24 hours ahead. Within 24 hours of the appointment you cannot cancel or reschedule, and canceling late or not showing up forfeits your exam fee. AWS may waive the fee for personal illness or an unforeseen emergency with documentation.

Source: [AWS Certification FAQs](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/faqs/), [Before Testing](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/before-testing/)

## Online proctored vs test center

You can take the exam at a Pearson VUE testing center or as an online proctored exam. The rules differ in ways that matter on the day:

- **Test center:** plan to arrive 15 to 30 minutes before your appointment to complete sign-in. Unscheduled breaks are allowed, but the exam timer keeps running during them.
- **Online proctored:** you can launch the exam up to 30 minutes before your scheduled time. If you are more than 15 minutes late, or your computer fails the system test, you cannot launch the exam and your fee is forfeited. You may not stand up or leave the camera's view for any reason during the exam. You also have to communicate with a proctor to complete an online appointment.

Source: [During testing (test day)](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/during-testing/), [Before Testing](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/before-testing/)

## What to have ready

**ID.** The first and last names on your ID must be identical to those in your AWS Certification Account. For an online proctored exam you need one primary ID. At a test center you need two primary IDs, or one primary and one secondary ID. You need valid government-issued identification. If you do not have a qualifying one from the country where you test (EU ID holders excepted), AWS requires a passport from your country of citizenship, and a secondary ID is still required. The rules differ for online and in-person testing, so check the current policy before exam day.

**System check (online).** Run Pearson VUE's system test on the computer you will use before your appointment. AWS's pages do not list the full workspace rules, so check Pearson VUE's current online testing requirements, linked from the AWS policy pages, well before exam day.

Source: [During testing (test day)](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/during-testing/), [Before Testing](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/before-testing/)

## How the exam is structured

The exam guide describes two question types:

- **Multiple choice:** one correct response and three incorrect responses (distractors).
- **Multiple response:** two or more correct responses out of five or more options. You must select all the correct responses to receive credit.

In the standard format, 50 questions affect your score and 15 are unscored questions that AWS is evaluating for future use. The unscored questions are not identified, so treat every question as if it counts. Unanswered questions are scored as incorrect, and there is no penalty for guessing. The beta has 85 questions in total.

The scored content is split across four domains: Data Preparation for ML and AI (28%), ML Model and Foundation Model (FM) Development (24%), Deployment and Orchestration of ML and AI Workflows (24%), and Operating, Monitoring, and Securing ML and AI Solutions (24%). The target candidate has at least one year of experience using Amazon SageMaker AI, Amazon Bedrock and other AWS services for ML engineering, with both traditional ML and generative AI.

Source: [AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer - Associate (MLA-C02) exam guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/machine-learning-engineer-associate-02/machine-learning-engineer-associate-02.html), [AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer - Associate](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-machine-learning-engineer-associate/)

## How scoring works

Results are reported as a scaled score from 100 to 1,000, and the minimum passing score is 720. Scaled scoring lets AWS compare results across exam forms that may differ slightly in difficulty, so 720 is a point on the scale, not "72% of questions correct."

The exam uses a compensatory scoring model. You do not need to pass each section, only the exam as a whole. Your score report may include a table of how you did in each section; treat it as a general signal of strengths and weaknesses. The exam guide notes that its pass or fail designation does not apply to the beta version, and points to AWS's general beta information.

Source: [AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer - Associate (MLA-C02) exam guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/machine-learning-engineer-associate-02/machine-learning-engineer-associate-02.html)

## Results and your digital badge

Most AWS Certification exams do not show a pass or fail result at the end of the test. Results are posted to your AWS Certification Account within five business days, and you can view, download or print the exam report from the Exam History table. For the MLA-C02 beta, AWS says results are typically available within 5 business days of exam completion.

If you pass, AWS issues a digital badge through Credly, and the Credly email can arrive before your results show up in your account. You can share the badge on social media and add it to a website or email signature. The certification is valid for three years, and you can recertify by passing the latest version of the exam before it expires.

Source: [AWS Certification FAQs](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/faqs/), [After Testing](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/after-testing/), [AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer - Associate](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/certified-machine-learning-engineer-associate/)

## If you don't pass

For a standard exam, you must wait 14 calendar days before you can retake it. There is no limit on attempts, but you pay the full registration fee each time. A beta can be taken only once; after that, you wait for the standard exam to be released before trying again.

Source: [After Testing](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/policies/after-testing/)

## On exam day: practical tips

- **Pace yourself.** Divide the time limit by the question count for your version and check your progress at regular points so you leave time to review.
- **Flag and move on.** If a question is taking too long, pick your best answer, flag it, and come back. Never leave a question blank, since a guess costs nothing.
- **Find the constraint.** Most MLA-C02 scenarios turn on one stated constraint, such as no idle cost, a synchronous caller, no labels, a new account, or least operational effort. Find it before reading the options.
- **Read multiple-response items carefully.** You only get credit if you select every correct response.
